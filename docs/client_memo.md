## **1. Recommendation: Prioritize franchise bundles, supplement with standalones**

**Make franchises the primary acquisition strategy, but retain a selective standalone budget.**

Our analysis of 112 distinct films from the original 127-title candidate list suggests that franchise bundles offer the stronger case for acquisition when the objective is maximizing profit per film and securing a portfolio of proven titles. Standalones still deserve a place because several deliver exceptional returns on smaller production budgets and stronger audience ratings.

The recommendation holds in both time windows when comparing average and median profit per acquisition unit and median return. However, the average return per dollar changes depending on the period: standalones lead across all years, while franchises lead from 2000 onward. Given Finance's concerns about older box-office data, I would use the modern window as the primary basis for the board's decision.

### **The evidence**

| Measure | All years: Franchise | All years: Standalone | 2000 onward: Franchise | 2000 onward: Standalone |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Acquisition units | 23 | 25 | 12 | 12 |
| Films represented | 87 | 25 | 44 | 12 |
| Average profit per film | $523M | $276M | $657M | $325M |
| Median profit per unit | $1.82B | $254M | $2.04B | $299M |
| Average ROI | 497.46% | 947.99% | 471.51% | 389.36% |
| Median ROI | 455.11% | 400.00% | 408.13% | 348.80% |
| Average revenue per $1 production cost | $5.97 | $10.48 | $5.72 | $4.89 |
| Median revenue per $1 production cost | $5.55 | $5.00 | $5.08 | $4.49 |
| Average audience rating | 7.45 | 8.10 | 7.52 | 7.92 |
| Median audience rating | 7.37 | 8.21 | 7.43 | 8.14 |

Profit and returns are based on historical box office and production budgets, not StreamVault licensing costs. Profit per film is calculated across the films represented within the acquisition units. All financial figures are nominal, not inflation-adjusted.

### **Why I recommend bundles**

- More profit per film: Modern franchise films averaged approximately $657M in estimated profit versus $325M for standalones in our sample.
- Better typical returns: The median franchise ROI was 408.13% versus 348.80% for modern standalones. Franchises also had a stronger median revenue-to-budget multiple of 5.08 versus 4.49.
- Portfolio depth: Bundles provide multiple titles that can support continued engagement and a larger content offering rather than relying on individual films.
- Standalones remain valuable: They had stronger average and median audience ratings and should be used to complement the bundle strategy, particularly where the acquisition cost is attractive.

### **The important qualification**

**The average return per dollar changes with the time window.**

- Across all years, standalone films average $10.48 in box-office revenue per $1 of production budget, against $5.97 for franchises.
- From 2000 onward, franchises average $5.72 against $4.89 for standalones.
- Three older standalone hits — Jaws, The Good, the Bad and the Ugly, and Pulp Fiction — materially influence the all-years standalone average.

If Finance excludes older titles, the modern comparison supports franchise bundles. If the board uses all historical data, standalones look better on average return per dollar. I would not conceal that distinction.

## **4. Deal note: The Lord of the Rings Collection**

The Lord of the Rings is our strongest franchise acquisition candidate. I would negotiate for it aggressively, but not at an unlimited premium.

The three-film collection generated approximately $2.65B in estimated historical profit, with an ROI of 996.45% and an average audience rating of 8.47. It earned approximately $10.96 in box-office revenue for every $1 spent on production, nearly double the typical modern franchise multiple of $5.08. This efficiency is the single number that most influenced our recommendation. It makes the collection an attractive target relative to the other bundles, despite The Avengers generating more total profit and Harry Potter offering more films. I would be willing to prioritize it over lower-ranked franchise opportunities, but I would not use historical box-office success as a justification for paying an unlimited licensing premium. I would also ask whether The Hobbit films are included in the proposed package. The Hobbit appears separately in our analysis, with a much lower revenue-to-budget multiple of approximately $3.91, so it should not automatically receive the same valuation as The Lord of the Rings trilogy.

## **5. What we could not verify**

These limitations matter because they affect how much confidence Kwame should place in the recommendation and how he should negotiate.

| Limitation | What it means for the decision |
|---|---|
| Licensing prices unavailable | We cannot establish how much StreamVault should pay for any title or how many titles the $14M can buy. Historical box-office success is not equivalent to streaming revenue or licensing value. |
| Candidate list incomplete after cleaning | The 127 candidate entries resulted in 112 distinct films for the analysis. Seven entries returned no film, one row was blank, and duplicates were merged. The resulting analysis does not represent every original entry. |
| Historical hit bias | The list is dominated by established titles with known box-office success. We cannot conclude that an average franchise or standalone acquisition would perform similarly. |
| Actual bundle contents may differ | Our bundle analysis reflects the films represented in the candidate data. Studios may offer additional films, including weaker entries, as mandatory bundle components. |
| Box office is not streaming performance | We do not have subscriber acquisition, viewing hours, retention, churn reduction or streaming-specific performance data. These could materially change the value of the titles. |
| Older financial figures | Historical figures are nominal and not adjusted for inflation. The older window is particularly sensitive to differences in reporting practices and historical market conditions. |
| Talent evidence is limited | Director and actor patterns are based on a small selected set of films. They should guide negotiation research, not determine acquisition decisions on their own. |
| Historical performance does not guarantee future returns | Popularity, audience demand and licensing value can change over time, and box-office results do not establish future streaming performance. |

| Stage | Count | Explanation |

The gap is not merely administrative. The analysis supports a decision among the usable films, not a claim that all 127 candidate entries were fully evaluated.

## **6. One improvement beyond the brief: A licensing negotiation scorecard**

Build a simple deal scorecard that converts this historical analysis into a negotiation tool.

The notebook tells us which films and franchises have historically performed well. What it does not tell us is whether the studio's asking price is a good deal for StreamVault.

I would add a small scorecard that Adjoa completes for every offer received, using the shortlist and the financial evidence already produced by the notebook.

| Field | What Adjoa records |
|---|---|
| Acquisition candidate | Franchise or standalone title |
| Studio asking price | Actual licence fee |
| Licence duration | Contract term |
| Titles included | Exact films in the proposed deal |
| Negotiated price | StreamVault's proposed fee |
| Historical performance | Profit, ROI and audience score from our analysis |
| Negotiation justification | Why the title or bundle merits the price |
| Decision | Accept, counteroffer or reject |

### **Why this is worth doing**

- For Kwame: It gives him a defensible way to challenge a studio's asking price instead of relying on box-office figures alone.
- For Finance: It makes the $14M allocation visible at the deal level, rather than presenting historical performance as if it were the actual cost of acquisition.
- For Adjoa: It is reusable every quarter. She can update the shortlist, enter new studio offers and use the same decision structure as the candidate list grows to 500 titles.

The value of the improvement is that it bridges the gap between identifying good films and actually spending the $14M wisely.
